About the Programme

MBA CET Preparation Built Around the Actual Exam

MBA CET tests four distinct skill areas — Logical Reasoning, Abstract Reasoning, Quantitative Aptitude, and Verbal Ability & Reading Comprehension — in a single 150-minute, 200-question window. Our Dombivli centre is built around that reality: structured practice across all four sections, disciplined mock-test pacing, and a faculty team who teach each section the way MBA CET actually tests it, not generic aptitude training.

Every student starts with a diagnostic assessment to get an honest baseline across all four sections and set realistic section-wise goals before structured teaching begins. That result shapes an individual study plan — concept-building where it's needed, topic-wise and sectional practice as fundamentals strengthen, and a pace suited to each student rather than one fixed track for the whole batch.

As preparation progresses, classroom teaching is paired with full-length mock tests that simulate the real MBA CET interface and 150-minute duration, followed by detailed performance analysis — because taking a mock is only the first step; understanding exactly why you scored what you scored is where the real improvement happens.

Exam Structure

MBA CET & the CAP Process

An MBA CET percentile feeds into Maharashtra's Centralized Admission Process (CAP) — the official counselling mechanism covering registration, document verification, merit list publication, choice filling and seat allotment across multiple rounds. A strong percentile makes a candidate eligible for shortlisting; it does not by itself guarantee a seat at any particular institute, since each college's effective cut-off depends on percentile, category, candidature type (Maharashtra State/All India) and seat availability in that round.

Because the score is only the entry point into CAP, our coaching doesn't stop at concept teaching. Sectional practice builds speed and accuracy across all four areas separately; full-length mocks replicate the real 150-minute, no-sectional-limit format so students can test and refine their own attempt order; and as MBA CET approaches, focus shifts to question-selection strategy and exam-day pacing.

Who Can Apply

Eligibility for MBA CET

A bachelor's degree of at least three years, in any discipline, from a recognised university is the baseline academic requirement, with minimum aggregate marks that vary by category. There is generally no upper age limit. Candidates should always verify current-year details on the official notification at cetcell.mahacet.org.

Graduation & Minimum Marks

General category candidates need at least 50% aggregate marks; candidates from backward class categories and PwD candidates belonging to Maharashtra State need at least 45%. Final-year students may apply provisionally, subject to completing their degree before admissions conclude.

Maharashtra State vs All India Candidature

Maharashtra State (MS) candidates who meet the state's domicile-related and educational requirements are eligible for reservation benefits and a wider range of CAP seats. All India (AI)/Outside-Maharashtra candidates can also appear and participate in CAP under the All India category, generally without state-specific reservation.

Work Experience & Age

Work experience is not mandatory to appear, though some institutes may give limited additional weightage during CAP. There is generally no upper age limit — always confirm current requirements before applying.

Syllabus

What MBA CET Actually Tests

The MBA CET syllabus is set by the State CET Cell, Maharashtra, and may be revised each admission cycle — the topics below reflect the major preparation areas across all four sections.

Logical Reasoning & Abstract Reasoning

Logical Reasoning carries the highest weightage in the paper (75 questions); Abstract Reasoning is entirely visual and pattern-based, and needs regular, dedicated practice to build familiarity and speed.

  • Arrangements & Puzzles — seating arrangements, blood relations, direction sense.
  • Coding & Series — coding-decoding, number and letter series, logical sequences.
  • Syllogisms & Statements — statement-conclusion, statement-assumption, cause and effect.
  • Figure Series & Patterns — pattern recognition, figural analogies, visual reasoning.
  • Figure Classification — odd-figure-out, embedded figures, missing figures.
  • Series Completion — non-verbal reasoning and series-based questions.

Quantitative Aptitude

Tests mathematical concepts and data interpretation, with a strong weightage in the overall paper (50 questions).

  • Number System & Arithmetic — percentages, profit & loss, ratio & proportion, averages.
  • Time-Based Topics — time and work, time-speed-distance, simple and compound interest.
  • Algebra & Geometry — algebra and equations, geometry and mensuration.
  • P&C, Probability & DI — permutation, combination, probability, and data interpretation (tables, charts, graphs).

Verbal Ability & Reading Comprehension

Tests English usage, comprehension and verbal reasoning (50 questions).

  • Reading Comprehension — main idea, inference, tone, contextual understanding.
  • Grammar & Vocabulary — sentence correction, sentence completion, synonyms and antonyms.
  • Idioms & Para-Based Questions — idioms and phrases, para-based questions, sentence arrangement.
  • Verbal Reasoning — contextual and inference-based verbal reasoning questions.
