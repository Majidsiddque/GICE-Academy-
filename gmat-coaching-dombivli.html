About the Programme

GMAT Preparation Built for Dombivli Aspirants

The GMAT Exam (Graduate Management Admission Test) is a standardised, computer-adaptive admissions test administered by the Graduate Management Admission Council (GMAC), used by business schools and universities worldwide to evaluate applicants to MBA, Master's in Management and other graduate management programmes.

The current GMAT Focus Edition assesses analytical reasoning, quantitative ability, verbal comprehension, and data-driven decision-making — skills that closely mirror the challenges of a business-school classroom. Because the GMAT rewards accuracy, reasoning speed, and strategic thinking rather than rote memorisation, structured and well-guided preparation makes a genuine difference.

Our Dombivli centre supports GMAT aspirants through concept building, guided and progressive practice, realistic mock tests, detailed performance analysis, and consistent faculty guidance and mentorship, following a simple philosophy: Learn → Practise → Test → Analyse → Improve → Perform.

Exam Structure

The Three GMAT Sections

The current GMAT Focus Edition is built around three core, computer-adaptive sections. Candidates can generally choose the order in which they attempt the three sections at the start of the exam.

Core Section
Quantitative Reasoning

Mathematical problem-solving covering arithmetic, algebra, problem solving, number properties and word problems.

Core Section
Verbal Reasoning

Reading comprehension and critical, argument-based reasoning — understanding arguments, assumptions and evidence.

Core Section
Data Insights

Data sufficiency, multi-source reasoning, table analysis, graphics interpretation and two-part analysis.

Adaptive
Computer-Adaptive Format

Question difficulty adjusts within each section based on performance; an on-screen calculator is available for Data Insights, and no separate AWA or Integrated Reasoning section is included in the current format.

Exact question counts, section timings and scoring details can be revised by GMAC. Always verify current details on the official mba.com website before finalising your preparation plan.

Who Can Apply

GMAT Eligibility Criteria

GMAC keeps GMAT eligibility deliberately broad — there is no fixed educational qualification, work-experience requirement, or nationality restriction to sit for the exam.

Age Requirements

Candidates aged 18 and above can register independently. Candidates aged 13-17 may be permitted with a parent or legal guardian's written consent. There is no upper age limit.

Educational Background

No specific undergraduate degree, academic stream or minimum GPA is required by GMAC. Most business schools, however, expect applicants to hold or be completing an undergraduate degree at the time of MBA admission.

Identity & Registration

A valid, original, government-issued photo ID is required at the time of the test, and registration is completed through the candidate's official mba.com account.

Retake Rules & Score Validity

Candidates can generally take the GMAT up to five times within any rolling 12-month period, with a required waiting period between attempts. Scores are typically valid for five years.

Individual MBA programmes may add their own requirements, such as a completed bachelor's degree, minimum work experience, or English-language proficiency scores. Always verify current details on the official mba.com website.

Syllabus

What the GMAT Sections Cover

The GMAT syllabus is organised around the three core sections of the current GMAT Focus Edition. Candidates should always confirm the latest topic list on the official GMAT website, as GMAC may refine content areas over time.

Quantitative Reasoning

Arithmetic (number properties, fractions, decimals, percentages, ratios, proportions), averages and statistics-based problem solving, algebra (equations, inequalities, expressions), and word problems requiring translation of real-world scenarios into mathematical reasoning.

Verbal Reasoning

Reading comprehension (main ideas, tone, author's purpose, detailed information) and critical reasoning (understanding arguments, identifying assumptions, drawing inferences, evaluating evidence and conclusions).

  • Data Insights — data sufficiency, multi-source reasoning, table analysis, graphics interpretation and two-part analysis, testing the ability to interpret and reason with data to make decisions.
  • No AWA or IR — the current format does not include a separate Analytical Writing Assessment or Integrated Reasoning section from earlier GMAT versions.
  • Flexible section order — candidates can generally choose the order in which they attempt the three sections.
  • Review & edit — the current format allows marking, revisiting and changing a limited number of answers within a section before moving on.

Common Questions

GMAT Coaching in Dombivli — FAQ

What is the GMAT exam?

The GMAT Exam is a standardised, computer-adaptive test administered by GMAC that assesses analytical, quantitative, verbal, and data-reasoning skills for admission to MBA and graduate management programmes worldwide.

What is the current GMAT exam pattern?

The current GMAT Focus Edition is a three-section, computer-adaptive format — Quantitative Reasoning, Verbal Reasoning and Data Insights — with flexible section ordering and a limited question review-and-edit option.

What is the GMAT eligibility criteria?

GMAT eligibility is broad — no fixed educational or work-experience requirement. Candidates generally must be at least 18 (or 13-17 with parental consent) and register with valid identification.

Can the GMAT be taken more than once?

Candidates can generally take the GMAT up to five times within any rolling 12-month period, with a required waiting period between attempts. Always verify exact rules on the official mba.com website.

Which business schools accept GMAT scores?

A large number of business schools in India and internationally accept the GMAT, though requirements and score expectations vary by institution. Candidates should verify directly with each target school.

Does GICE Academy in Dombivli help with GMAT mock tests and sectional practice?

Yes — sectional tests, topic tests and full-length mock tests simulating the real adaptive format, along with detailed performance analysis, are built into the preparation journey alongside subject-wise classroom teaching.
