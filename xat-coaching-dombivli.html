About the Programme

XAT Preparation Built for Dombivli Aspirants

XAT (Xavier Aptitude Test) is a national-level management entrance examination conducted by XLRI Jamshedpur on behalf of XAMI (the Xavier Association of Management Institutes), used for admission to MBA/PGDM and related management programmes at XLRI and a large network of other participating business schools.

What sets XAT apart is its unique Decision Making section, along with a distinctive marking scheme and a General Knowledge component. Because the exam rewards genuine reasoning ability rather than rote learning, structured and well-guided preparation makes a real difference to how a candidate ultimately performs.

Our Dombivli centre supports XAT aspirants through strong concept building, guided practice, dedicated Decision Making preparation, realistic mock tests, detailed performance analysis, and consistent faculty guidance and mentorship, following a simple philosophy: Learn → Practise → Test → Analyse → Improve → Repeat.

Exam Structure

The Four XAT Sections

XAT is organised into two parts: Part 1 combines three sections within a single overall time limit, and Part 2 is a separately timed General Knowledge section.

Part 1
Verbal Ability & Logical Reasoning

Around 26 questions covering reading comprehension, vocabulary, grammar, para jumbles and critical reasoning.

Part 1 — Distinctive
Decision Making

Around 21 questions of situational and case-based analytical decision-making — XAT's defining, most distinctive section.

Part 1
Quantitative Ability & DI

Around 28 questions covering quantitative aptitude, data interpretation and analysis.

Part 2 — ~10 min
General Knowledge

Around 20 questions on current affairs and static awareness, separately timed and not counted toward the shortlisting percentile in recent years, but factored into final selection.

Exact question counts, timing and marking rules can be revised year to year. Always verify current details on the official XAT website (xatonline.in) before finalising your preparation plan.

Who Can Apply

XAT Eligibility Criteria

XAT eligibility criteria are set by XLRI Jamshedpur and are generally consistent year to year, though candidates should always confirm the latest official notification before applying.

Educational Qualification

A bachelor's degree of at least three years' duration (or equivalent) in any discipline from a recognised university. Candidates with certain professional qualifications (CA, CS, ICWA/CMA) are typically also eligible.

Final-Year Students

Candidates in their final year, or those awaiting final results, are generally also eligible, subject to submitting proof of degree completion by a specified deadline set by XLRI.

Age Criteria

There is generally no upper or lower age limit specified for appearing in the XAT examination itself.

NRI & International Candidates

NRI, OCI and foreign national candidates seeking admission to XLRI's programmes are typically required to apply through GMAT (or, in some cases, GRE) scores rather than XAT.

Meeting XAT eligibility allows you to sit for the exam, but each institute may set its own additional academic requirements for final admission. Always verify current details on xatonline.in.

Syllabus

What the XAT Sections Cover

The XAT syllabus is organised broadly around four areas. Not every topic appears in every year's paper — these represent the major areas candidates should prepare for.

Verbal Ability & Logical Reasoning

Reading comprehension (including varied and sometimes unconventional passage types), vocabulary and grammar, sentence correction and completion, para jumbles, critical reasoning and argument evaluation, and inference-based questions.

Decision Making

Situational judgement based on business or workplace scenarios, ethical decision-making, case-based and caselet-driven reasoning, analytical decision-making involving multiple variables, prioritisation of competing objectives, and structured evaluation of possible options.

  • Quantitative Ability & DI — number system, arithmetic, percentages, profit and loss, time and work, algebra, geometry, mensuration, probability, permutation-combination, and data interpretation involving tables, charts and graphs.
  • General Knowledge — current affairs (national and international, business, government initiatives) and static GK (history, geography, polity, economy, organisations).
  • Decision Making as its own skill — best treated as a distinct area, not simply an extension of logical reasoning.
  • GK role in selection — not counted toward the overall percentile in recent years, but factored into final selection by XLRI and other institutes.

XAT Examination Pattern

The figures below are indicative of the recent-year XAT structure. Always verify the exact current-year structure on the official XAT website (xatonline.in) closer to your test date.

XAT Section-Wise Pattern (Recent-Year Reference)
SectionApprox. QuestionsMajor Areas
Verbal Ability & Logical Reasoning (Part 1)~26Reading comprehension, grammar, critical reasoning
Decision Making (Part 1)~21Situational & case-based analytical decision-making
Quantitative Ability & DI (Part 1)~28Quant aptitude, data interpretation
General Knowledge (Part 2)~20Current affairs, static GK — separately timed
  • Marking scheme — recent cycles: +1 for correct, negative marking for incorrect, and a small additional penalty for excess unattempted questions in Part 1. GK has typically carried no negative marking.
  • Total duration — around three hours (180 minutes) in recent cycles.
  • No sectional timer within Part 1 — candidates can allocate time across Verbal Ability, Decision Making and Quant/DI as they see fit within the overall Part 1 window.
  • Part 2 is separately timed — around 10 minutes for General Knowledge.

XLRI can revise the pattern, question counts, timing or marking scheme year to year. Always verify the latest official notification and exam-pattern page on xatonline.in before building a detailed preparation plan.

Common Questions

XAT Coaching in Dombivli — FAQ

What is the XAT exam?

XAT (Xavier Aptitude Test) is a national-level management entrance examination conducted by XLRI Jamshedpur on behalf of XAMI, used for admission to XLRI and a wide network of other participating MBA/PGDM institutes.

What is the XAT exam pattern?

XAT is a computer-based test with objective questions across four sections — Verbal Ability & Logical Reasoning, Decision Making, Quantitative Ability & DI, and a separately timed General Knowledge section — with negative marking.

What is the XAT eligibility criteria?

Candidates generally need a bachelor's degree of at least three years' duration (or equivalent) in any discipline; final-year students are typically also eligible, subject to submitting proof of completion by a specified deadline.

What is Decision Making in XAT?

Decision Making is a distinctive XAT section that evaluates how well candidates can reason through realistic business and ethical scenarios and choose the most appropriate course of action, rather than testing memorised formulas.

Does XLRI accept XAT?

Yes, XAT is the primary entrance examination used by XLRI Jamshedpur for its flagship MBA/PGDM programmes, along with a wide network of other participating institutes across India.

Does GICE Academy in Dombivli help with XAT mock tests and Decision Making practice?

Yes — sectional tests, topic tests, dedicated Decision Making caselet practice, and full-length mock tests across all sections, along with detailed performance analysis, are built into the preparation journey.
