Exam Structure

CAT Admission Journey

A strong CAT percentile makes a candidate eligible for shortlisting — it does not by itself guarantee admission. After the exam, each institute applies its own shortlisting criteria using the percentile alongside academic profile, work experience, and category, before moving candidates into further rounds such as the Written Ability Test (WAT), Group Discussion (GD) or Personal Interview (PI).

Because the exam itself is only the first filter, our coaching doesn't stop at concept teaching. Sectional sessions build VARC, DILR and QA speed and accuracy separately; mock-test phases replicate the strict, non-navigable 40-minute-per-section format; and as CAT approaches, focus shifts to question-selection strategy and exam-day time management, since the final call also depends heavily on what happens after the percentile is out.

Who Can Apply

Eligibility for the CAT Exam

A Bachelor's degree (minimum three years) from a recognised university is the baseline academic requirement, with minimum aggregate marks that vary by category. There is no upper age limit and no cap on the number of attempts. Candidates should always verify current-year details on the official CAT information bulletin at iimcat.ac.in.

Graduation & Minimum Marks

General, EWS and NC-OBC candidates need at least 50% aggregate marks (or equivalent CGPA); SC, ST and PwBD candidates need at least 45%. Professional-degree holders (CA, CS, ICWA/CMA, FIAI) are also eligible, subject to applicable minimum marks.

Final-Year & Result-Awaited Students

Candidates in their final year, or awaiting results, can also apply — if shortlisted, they must submit a certificate confirming degree completion within the admitting institute's timeline.

Age, Attempts & Reservation

No upper age limit and no restriction on number of attempts. Reserved-category candidates must upload valid category certificates at registration, as per Government of India norms.

Syllabus

What CAT Actually Tests

The IIMs don't release an official, fixed CAT syllabus — the topics below reflect the consistent pattern of questions asked over recent years, across three sections.

VARC — Verbal Ability & Reading Comprehension

Tests reading speed, comprehension, inference and command over English usage. Reading Comprehension typically forms the larger share of this section.

  • Reading Comprehension — passages from economy, science, history, philosophy and culture.
  • Inference & Tone — main idea, central argument, author's tone, critical reading of arguments.
  • Para Jumbles & Para Summary — arranging jumbled sentences, identifying the best summary.
  • Odd Sentence & Vocabulary — out-of-context sentence identification, sentence completion.

DILR — Data Interpretation & Logical Reasoning

Combines analytical reasoning with reading and interpreting data across connected sets of questions.

  • Tables, Charts & Graphs — bar, line and pie chart-based data comparison and caselets.
  • Arrangements & Puzzles — seating, logical arrangements, and logical sets.
  • Venn Diagrams & Set-Based Reasoning — games, tournaments, routes, networks, scheduling.
  • Mixed Reasoning Sets — other data-based reasoning formats seen in recent papers.

QA — Quantitative Ability

Tests mathematical concepts largely at the Class 10–12 level, with emphasis on application and speed over advanced theory.

  • Arithmetic — percentages, profit and loss, ratio and proportion, averages, time-speed-distance, time and work.
  • Number System & Interest — simple and compound interest, core number-system concepts.
  • Algebra & Equations — algebraic manipulation and equation-solving.
  • Geometry, Mensuration & P&C — geometry, mensuration, permutation, combination and probability.

Do you offer online classes?

Can working professionals join?

Yes. We run dedicated weekend and evening batches specifically for candidates balancing a full-time job with CAT preparation, without compressing the syllabus.

How often are mock tests conducted?

Full-length mock CATs run at regular intervals through the programme, timed and formatted to match the actual exam's sectional structure, with detailed post-mock analysis.
