About the Programme

SSC GD Preparation — Academic and Physical, Together

SSC GD (General Duty) is a national-level exam conducted by the Staff Selection Commission to select Constable and equivalent posts in the Central Armed Police Forces — BSF, CRPF, CISF, SSB, ITBP, Assam Rifles and others notified for the cycle. Set at the Matriculation (Class 10) level, it's one of India's largest annual recruitment drives.

Because SSC GD combines a written Computer-Based Examination with the Physical Efficiency Test (PET) and Physical Standard Test (PST), success depends on academic preparation and genuine physical readiness working together — not one at the expense of the other. Our coaching builds both from the start of a student's preparation, not physical training as an afterthought once the CBT is cleared.

Preparation follows the cycle: Learn → Practise → Test → Analyse → Improve → Train → Repeat, with physical-test awareness — understanding current PET/PST standards and tracking progress against them — built in alongside subject teaching.

Who Can Apply

SSC GD Eligibility

Educational Qualification

Candidates must have passed Matriculation / Class 10th from a recognised board — no additional academic qualification beyond Class 10 is required.

Age Limit (Current Cycle)

General (unreserved) age limit is 18 to 23 years, calculated as on the reference date in the official notification. Category-wise relaxation applies — OBC +3 years, SC/ST +5 years, Ex-Servicemen up to 28 years, as per current rules.

Nationality & Domicile

Candidates must be Indian citizens as per the citizenship requirements in the official notification. Domicile-related requirements, where applicable, are specified force-wise and State/UT-wise.

Eligibility requirements, age relaxation and domicile rules can differ between recruitment cycles and forces. Always verify every detail from the current official SSC GD notification at ssc.gov.in before applying.

Syllabus

SSC GD Syllabus Subject-Wise

The syllabus is set at the Matriculation (Class 10) level, divided into four sections tested in the Computer-Based Examination.

  • General Intelligence & Reasoning — analogy, classification, series, coding-decoding, Venn diagrams, blood relations, non-verbal and spatial reasoning.
  • General Knowledge & General Awareness — current affairs, Indian history, geography, polity, economy, general science, government schemes, static GK.
  • Elementary Mathematics — number system, percentage, ratio & proportion, average, interest, profit & loss, mensuration, time & distance, time & work.
  • English / Hindi — basic comprehension, vocabulary, grammar, sentence understanding, reading ability.

The topics above reflect the general SSC GD syllabus pattern. Candidates must refer to the official syllabus PDF released with the current notification for the exact, cycle-specific topic list.

Exam Pattern

SSC GD Computer-Based Examination

The CBT generally comprises 80 objective-type questions carrying 160 marks, attempted within 60 minutes, with negative marking for incorrect answers — questions are distributed roughly equally across the four subjects.

Physical Requirements

PET & PST Readiness

Physical assessment is as critical as the written exam. Standards can vary by gender, category, region and the specific rules of the current cycle.

Physical Efficiency Test (PET)

A timed race event assessing fitness, stamina and endurance. Requirements — distance and qualifying time — vary for male and female candidates, with relaxed standards for regions such as Ladakh, as notified. Ex-servicemen are generally exempted from the race but must appear for measurement.

Physical Standard Test (PST)

Verifies height, chest measurement (male candidates, including expansion) and other prescribed measurements. Category- and region-specific relaxations may apply. Weight-based rejection has reportedly shifted to a BMI-based assessment during the medical exam — verify current rules officially.

Medical Examination

Assesses general physical and medical fitness, vision standards and BMI. Candidates found unfit may request a Review Medical Examination (RME), subject to applicable rules and timelines.

Do not rely on measurements or standards from previous recruitment cycles. Physical standards, relaxation provisions and medical criteria must be verified from the current official SSC GD notification and PET/PST guidelines. This section is general awareness, not medical or fitness advice — consult a qualified professional for individualised guidance.

Where It Leads

Careers After SSC GD

SSC GD recruits Constable (General Duty) and equivalent posts across forces notified for the current cycle — the exact force list can change year to year.

Border & Internal Security

BSF, SSB, ITBP and Assam Rifles for border security; CRPF for internal security and law-and-order support.

Installation & Specialised Security

CISF for installation security, SSF for designated government premises, and Sepoy posts in the Narcotics Control Bureau (NCB).

Force allocation depends on final merit, vacancies and candidate preferences — not on CBT score alone. SSC GD Constable posts generally fall under Pay Level-3 of the 7th CPC for most forces; NCB Sepoy posts fall under a different pay level. Verify current figures from the official notification.

Common Questions

SSC GD Coaching — FAQ

What is the SSC GD exam?

A national recruitment examination conducted by SSC to select candidates for Constable (General Duty) posts in Central Armed Police Forces and other notified organisations.

What is the minimum qualification for SSC GD?

Candidates must have passed Matriculation / Class 10th from a recognised board — no additional academic qualification is required.

What are the physical requirements for SSC GD?

Candidates must meet the Physical Standard Test (height, chest for males, other measurements) and clear the Physical Efficiency Test (a timed race event), as per current official standards.

Is there negative marking in the SSC GD CBT?

Yes — negative marking applies for incorrect answers; sources vary between 0.25 and 0.50 marks for the current cycle, so confirm the exact deduction from the official notification.

What happens after the written exam?

Candidates meeting the qualifying marks in the CBT are shortlisted for PET and PST, followed by Medical Examination and Document Verification.

Does GICE Academy help with physical preparation too?

Yes — GICE Academy provides physical-test awareness and readiness guidance alongside academic coaching, though it does not claim to provide professional physical training.
