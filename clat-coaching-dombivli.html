About the Programme

CLAT Preparation Built for Dombivli Aspirants

The CLAT Entrance Exam is the gateway that hundreds of thousands of students across India compete for every year — a single common test whose score, and the rank it produces, National Law Universities rely on for admission through centralised counselling. Our Dombivli centre exists to help law aspirants in the area prepare for that test the way it's actually structured: reading-heavy, passage-based, and reasoning-driven across all five sections.

Every student who joins begins with a diagnostic assessment, not a formality — a genuine read on where they stand in reading speed, comprehension, reasoning and pace. That baseline shapes the study plan we hand them, so preparation follows the roadmap Learn → Read → Practise → Test → Analyse → Improve → Revise, rather than random, unplanned study.

Classroom teaching is paired with section-wise practice, timed passage work, previous-year questions, and full-length mock tests conducted under exam-like conditions — the repetition that turns comprehension and reasoning ability into genuine exam-day readiness.

Our Approach

Reading-First Preparation

Legal & Logical Reasoning From Scratch

No prior law background is assumed. Legal Reasoning and Logical Reasoning are taught as learnable skills — principle-to-fact application and argument analysis — built through repeated, reviewed practice.

Analysis Beyond the Score

Every mock test is followed by detailed performance analysis covering reading errors, conceptual errors, calculation errors and guessing errors, feeding straight into targeted follow-up practice.

Who Can Apply

CLAT UG Eligibility

Candidates who have passed, or are appearing for, their Class 12 (or equivalent) examination and meet the current eligibility conditions prescribed by the Consortium of NLUs can apply for CLAT UG — from any stream, Science, Commerce or Arts/Humanities.

Educational Qualification

A Class 12 (10+2) pass, or equivalent, from a recognised board. Students appearing for board exams in the year of CLAT can apply, subject to proof of passing at the time of admission.

Minimum Percentage

Recent cycles have generally required around 45% aggregate for General/OBC/other categories and around 40% for SC/ST candidates, with the exact figure fixed by the Consortium of NLUs each year.

Age Limit

In recent cycles the Consortium of NLUs has not prescribed an upper age limit for CLAT UG — always confirm the current position from the official notification.

Eligibility percentages, age conditions and category-wise relaxations can change between examination cycles. Always verify current details against the latest official CLAT notification published by the Consortium of NLUs.

Syllabus

What the CLAT Exam Actually Tests

The CLAT UG syllabus is built around five sections, each almost entirely passage-based, so strong reading comprehension supports performance across the whole paper.

English Language

Comprehension passages drawn from contemporary and historically significant writing, testing reading comprehension, vocabulary in context, main idea, tone, inference and summary.

Current Affairs & General Knowledge

Passage-based questions drawing on national and international events, government policies and schemes, the economy, science and technology, and legal or constitutional developments in the news.

  • Legal Reasoning — application of stated legal principles to given facts; no prior law background required.
  • Logical Reasoning — arguments, premises, conclusions, assumptions, and strengthening or weakening a position.
  • Quantitative Techniques — basic arithmetic, ratios, percentages, averages, and data interpretation, generally up to Class 10 level.
  • Passage-Based Format — nearly every question across all five sections is built around a short passage or fact situation.
Marking Scheme

CLAT UG Exam Pattern

Recent CLAT UG cycles have used a single-day, single-paper, offline objective-type test of 120 multiple-choice questions across five sections, completed in a single two-hour sitting. All sections are compulsory, and scores from all sections combine into a single overall score — there is generally no sectional cut-off.

CLAT UG — Indicative Section-Wise Distribution
SectionIndicative Weightage*What It Tests
English Language~20%Reading comprehension, vocabulary, inference
Current Affairs incl. GK~25%Awareness of recent events and general knowledge
Legal Reasoning~25%Application of legal principles to facts
Logical Reasoning~20%Arguments, assumptions, critical reasoning
Quantitative Techniques~10%Basic mathematics and data interpretation

*Indicative, approximate proportions based on recent CLAT UG papers — not an official, fixed weightage.

  • Total questions — recent cycles have used 120 MCQs (reduced from 150 earlier).
  • Marking scheme — +1 for a correct answer, -0.25 for an incorrect one, no penalty for unattempted questions.
  • Duration — two hours (120 minutes) for all five sections in one sitting.
  • Exam mode — recent cycles have been conducted offline, in pen-and-paper mode.

The Consortium of NLUs confirms the exact number of questions, marks and any structural change for each cycle in its official notification. Treat the figures above as a preparation guide and verify current-year figures officially before finalising your strategy.

Where CLAT Leads

Colleges & Careers After CLAT

CLAT UG scores are accepted by around 25 National Law Universities, along with a number of other participating institutions and affiliated law colleges, for five-year integrated programmes such as BA LL.B., BBA LL.B., B.Com LL.B. and B.Sc. LL.B.

Law Courses After CLAT

BA LL.B. (Hons.) · BBA LL.B. (Hons.) · B.Com LL.B. (Hons.) · B.Sc. LL.B. (Hons.) and other integrated programmes, depending on the university.

Career Pathways

Litigation, corporate law, legal advisory, law firms, compliance, legal research, policy work, judicial services, civil services, and academia — each requiring its own further qualifications.

Common Questions

CLAT Coaching in Dombivli — FAQ

What is the CLAT Entrance Exam?

CLAT, the Common Law Admission Test, is a national-level examination conducted by the Consortium of National Law Universities for admission to law programmes at participating NLUs.

What is the CLAT syllabus?

Five sections — English Language, Current Affairs including General Knowledge, Legal Reasoning, Logical Reasoning, and Quantitative Techniques — almost entirely passage-based.

Is there negative marking in CLAT?

Yes. Recent cycles have deducted 0.25 marks for every incorrect answer, with no penalty for questions left unattempted.

Do I need a law background to prepare for Legal Reasoning?

No. Legal Reasoning tests the application of a stated principle to given facts — it's a learnable skill built through practice, not prior legal knowledge.

Can Class 11 & 12 students join CLAT coaching in Dombivli?

Yes — our schedules are built to sit alongside board-exam preparation, with reading habits and reasoning skills introduced gradually rather than all at once.

How important are CLAT mock tests?

Very. They build familiarity with the exam format, sharpen time management, and — when analysed properly — reveal exactly where a student needs to improve.
