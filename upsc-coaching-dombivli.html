About the Programme

UPSC Preparation Built Around the Actual Exam

Clearing the Civil Services Examination isn't about how many hours a candidate studies — it's about whether those hours are pointed at what UPSC actually tests. Our Dombivli centre exists precisely for that reason: a batch structure, a testing rhythm, and a faculty team all built around the real shape of the exam, not a generic "cover everything" syllabus dump.

Every student who joins us goes through a short diagnostic phase first — not a formality, but a genuine read on where they stand across the four core areas UPSC weighs most: static subject knowledge, current affairs application, answer-writing discipline, and time management under pressure. That diagnostic shapes the study plan we hand them, so no two students are on an identical track from day one.

Over the course of the programme, classroom teaching is paired with weekly writing practice, peer-reviewed answer evaluation, and structured revision cycles — the kind of repetition that turns knowledge into exam-ready recall rather than something read once and forgotten.

Exam Structure

UPSC Selection Stages

Candidates move through three genuinely selective stages, not formalities layered on top of each other. Clearing the Preliminary exam qualifies a candidate for the Mains; clearing the Mains qualifies them for the Personality Test, which decides final selection and service allocation. Each stage tests something different — the Prelims rewards breadth and speed, the Mains rewards depth and structured writing, and the Interview evaluates temperament and judgment as much as knowledge.

Because each stage demands a different skill, our coaching doesn't treat them as one continuous block of study. Prelims-focused sessions emphasize rapid recall and elimination technique; Mains sessions shift almost entirely to answer construction and time discipline; and Interview preparation, closer to the date, moves into mock panels and current-affairs discussion rounds.

Who Can Apply

Eligibility for the UPSC Exam

A bachelor's degree from a recognised university is the baseline academic requirement. Beyond that, UPSC sets age and nationality conditions, with relaxations for reserved categories, to keep the process fair and accessible to candidates from different backgrounds.

Graduation

A degree from a university recognised by the Government of India (or an equivalent qualifying credential) is required before appearing for the Mains — final-year students can appear for Prelims provisionally.

Age & Nationality

General category candidates typically fall between 21 and 32 years, with age relaxations for reserved categories. Candidates must meet UPSC's nationality requirements, generally Indian citizenship.

Subject Grounding

A working knowledge across Polity, History, Geography, Economy, Environment, and Current Affairs is expected — this is exactly what our syllabus-mapped coaching is built to establish and reinforce over the course.

Syllabus

What the Exam Actually Covers

Preliminary Exam

The Prelims tests general awareness, conceptual clarity, and applied reasoning rather than rote recall — it's designed to be a filter, not a comprehensive assessment, so speed and elimination skill matter as much as raw knowledge.

  • Current Affairs — national and international events, geopolitics, socio-economic developments.
  • History — ancient, medieval, and modern India, including the freedom movement.
  • Geography — physical, social, and economic geography of India and the world.
  • Indian Polity & Governance — the Constitution, political system, Panchayati Raj, public policy.
  • Economic & Social Development — poverty, inclusion, demographics, government schemes.
  • Environment & Ecology — biodiversity and climate change at a general-awareness level.
  • General Science — everyday applications of science and technology.

Mains Exam

The Mains demands depth and the ability to write structured, analytical answers under time pressure. It spans seven papers in total, and success here has less to do with knowing more facts and much more to do with organizing them persuasively within a strict word and time limit.

  • Essay — tests coherence and critical thinking across chosen topics.
  • General Studies I — Indian heritage, culture, history, and world geography.
  • General Studies II — governance, Constitution, polity, social justice, international relations.
  • General Studies III — technology, economic development, environment, security, disaster management.
  • General Studies IV — ethics, integrity, and aptitude.
  • Optional Subject (2 papers) — a subject of the candidate's choosing, tested in depth.

Can working professionals join?

Yes. We run dedicated weekend and evening batches specifically for candidates balancing a full-time job with UPSC preparation, without compressing the syllabus.
